**Agent:** I've handed back a plan for #289, and it is a split into four stories rather than a single pull request. It passes both hand-back checks, but no tests are written yet: each story's own planner writes them once the stories are filed. The split is needed because the issue needs more than five acceptance criteria and touches the checker, the cards, the river (the code that decides which step runs next) and all three prompts.

**The four stories**
1. **Kinds and table in code.** The checker accepts only three kinds of raise: question, blocker and issue. Who can raise what to whom is one fixed table in code. Every blocker must name who fixes it, every answer must say done or disagree with a reason, and a turn that skips a raise sent to it is rejected. Nothing in a hand-back can add a new kind or a new table entry.
2. **One Raised section on cards** (needs 1). Each line shows its kind's icon and who it is for. The old separate sections go, and a run that raised nothing shows no Raised section. Records already posted are still drawn into it, so old cards keep working.
3. **Every hand-back moves to the two fields** (needs 1 and 2). The planner, worker and reviewer use raises and answers, and the checker rejects any of the ten old fields by name. A worker's blocker for the planner goes through the reviewer first. On autopilot, the reviewer answers a question for you only with your own words as evidence. The three-blocks-in-a-row stop stays. All three prompts get the same section on raising and answering, with an example of each kind.
4. **Issue raises filed by code** (needs 3).

The order keeps main working after each story: the cards learn to draw raises before any hand-back starts producing them.

**Questions for you.** The plan goes ahead on these readings unless you say otherwise:
- **When the reviewer raises something to you:** I assumed it is always a question. Blockers go only to the planner or the worker, and "escalate" stays a review verdict.
- **Old fields that don't fit the three kinds:** I assumed a worker's suspect test or out-of-scope change becomes a blocker for the planner, sent through the reviewer. A change outside the plan becomes a question for you. A note worth keeping becomes an issue, and the other notes are dropped.
